1 · Manufacturing

Smaller nodes cost more carbon and water per die

The same 100 mm² die fabricated on Taiwan's grid, from 28 nm to 3 nm. More lithography and etch steps raise fab electricity, fluorinated process gases and ultrapure water per cm², and yield falls as defect density rises.

5 · AI audit

Greenwashing caught before it reaches a buyer's Scope 3

Auditor findings, written on-chain

How the verdict is reached

  • Physics: claimed vs. independent ACT estimate for the lot.
  • Energy floor: reported kWh × grid intensity is a lower bound.
  • IoT evidence: device-signed abatement and power meters.
  • Anomaly model: IsolationForest over intensity residuals.
  • Claude: reads documents and writes findings, but cannot overrule physics. A prompt-injection document was rejected in testing.

8 · Fab to inference

Over its life, running the chip outweighs making it, by far

Cleaner fabs matter, but most of an AI accelerator's lifetime footprint is set by where, when and how efficiently it runs. That is why GreenSilicon follows the chip into the data center. On very clean grids the embodied share rises sharply, which is why the passport matters there too.

12 · Energy per AI question

From 0.3 Wh to a fraction of that, per question

Baseline is about 0.3 Wh per typical chatbot query (Epoch AI); Google reports 0.24 Wh and 0.26 mL for its median Gemini prompt. Levers compound: semantic cache 30 %, half of the queries routed to a distilled model, speculative decoding 1.6×, quantization 0.85×, 20 % shorter answers.

13 · Cooling water

Can seawater replace drinking water? Yes, on the coast.

Five cooling strategies compared at three sites for a 5,000 MWh/year IT load. Seawater heat exchange is proven at scale (Google Hamina since 2011, PUE 1.09; Microsoft Natick, PUE 1.07), but needs a coastal site, corrosion-proof hardware and care with warm-water discharge.
