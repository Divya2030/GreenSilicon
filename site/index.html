The problem

AI is growing on chips nobody can verify the footprint of

Making a chip is one of the most energy-, gas- and water-intensive manufacturing processes on Earth, and running it in a data center keeps consuming electricity and water every hour. Buyers report these emissions using averages and unverifiable PDFs.

Opaque manufacturing

Each new node needs more electricity, more fluorinated process gases and more ultrapure water per cm². In our model a 3 nm die emits the carbon of the same die at 28 nm.

Fragmented supply chains

An AI accelerator combines chiplets from several fabs, HBM from a memory maker, packaging in another country and air freight. Nobody sees the whole footprint, and carbon is easily counted twice or not at all.

Hidden water and energy in AI

Every AI question and training run draws power and cooling water. In Phoenix, of an AI job's water is consumed at the power plant, not the data center, so it is invisible in most reports.

The solution

One passport per chip lot, verified at every step

Five smart contracts, a physics-based carbon model built on peer-reviewed data, and a hybrid AI auditor make every number traceable and every claim checkable.

DesignEstimate embodied carbon, energy and water before tape-out; mint a passport that commits to the design.
Fab & packageSuppliers sign EIP-712 disclosures; Claude extracts the figures from their PDFs.
AI + IoT auditPhysics, device-signed meters, an anomaly model and Claude vote. Greenwashing gets rejected on-chain.
ComposeChiplet carbon rolls into the product exactly once; credits are burned against lots.
Data centerEach AI job gets a certificate with energy, water and amortised chip carbon; schedulers pick clean hours and sites.
Reuse & recycleIdentical work is never computed twice; recyclers earn tokens for recovered metals.
