Who is already working on this, and what is still missing

The industry is moving: most leading chipmakers have net-zero targets and a consortium is building shared standards. But per-chip, verified, water-inclusive data that follows a chip into the data center does not exist yet. Every claim below links to its source; anything we could not verify is marked as such.

AI accelerators specifically

Only a handful of AI accelerators have a published carbon footprint

What has been disclosed

  • NVIDIA HGX H100 (8-GPU baseboard): 1,312 kg CO₂e cradle-to-gate, ISO 14067, HBM 42 % of the total. Excludes use phase. PCF summary
  • NVIDIA HGX B200: 24 % lower embodied carbon per exaflop than H100. NVIDIA blog
  • Google TPUs: five generations; operational electricity is over 70 % of lifetime emissions. Schneider, Patterson et al., 2025
  • AMD MI300X, NVIDIA GB200/GB300: no per-product footprint found (unverified).

How GreenSilicon's model compares

GreenSilicon's ACT-based estimate for its NX-7 accelerator is per accelerator, the same order of magnitude as NVIDIA's H100 board (~164 kg per GPU including board components). The lifecycle split agrees with Google's TPU study: operation dominates.

A limitation we found and disclose: NVIDIA attributes 42 % of the H100 board's footprint to HBM memory. Our model, using ACT's DRAM factor, gives HBM about , so it likely underestimates memory. Calibrating the memory model against published PCFs is first on our roadmap.

The AI and data-center side

Hyperscalers are disclosing more, but at fleet level

Per-query disclosures

Google: 0.24 Wh, 0.03 gCO₂e and 0.26 mL water per median Gemini prompt (2025), with its scope questioned by researchers (coverage). Mistral Large 2: 1.14 gCO₂e and 45 mL per 400-token reply (LCA).

Water pledges

Microsoft reports reaching water-positive in 2025 (ESG Today); AWS and Meta target 2030 (AWS, Meta). These are company-wide replenishment totals, not tied to hardware or jobs.

Zero-water cooling

Microsoft's designs since August 2024 use closed-loop chip-level cooling with no evaporation, avoiding over 125 million litres per data center per year (Microsoft). Google Hamina has used seawater since 2011.
